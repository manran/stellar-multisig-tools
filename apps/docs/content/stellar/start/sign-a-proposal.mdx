---
title: Sign a Proposal
description: Review one exact Stellar transaction and add your signature from your own wallet.
---

A Proposal is the shared view around one exact Stellar transaction. Review what it does, then add your signature from your own wallet.

## Shortest successful path

1. **Open the Proposal.** Follow the shared link or open it from **Inbox**. On Testnet, the link looks like `https://stellar-testnet.multisig.tools/s#<request-id>`. You do not need to create a Treasury to sign.
2. **Review the transaction.** Check the source account, recipients, assets, amounts, memo, and any signing-policy changes.
3. **Sign.** Connect the wallet that has signing authority. Use **Go to signing** to reach the signing section, then select **Sign**. Your wallet signs the exact transaction.
4. **Wait or submit.** The Proposal shows what approval power is still missing. When authorization is complete and execution is available, review the final submission details and select **Submit transaction**.
5. **Check the result.** After confirmation, select **View receipt**. Review the ledger, transaction hash, and collected signatures.

**Sign is not Submit.** Signing authorizes one exact transaction. It does not broadcast it or give MultiSig Tools custody of your private key.

## After submission

The Proposal displays **Confirmed on Stellar** once the network confirms the transaction. Open **View receipt** to inspect the record. Select **Print or save PDF** to open your browser's print dialog, then choose Save as PDF if needed. **Back to transaction** returns to the Proposal.

## Wallet connection and private access

**Connect wallet** selects the Stellar account used for an action. **Confirm wallet** proves control of that wallet to open private views such as Inbox and Templates. Opening a shared Proposal does not require a new MST account.

## Technical details

MultiSig Tools stores a technical Request around the Proposal so signatures and retained History can be coordinated. The transaction body is XDR; wallet signatures remain Stellar transaction signatures over that exact body.

If the transaction changes, existing signatures do not authorize the changed transaction.
